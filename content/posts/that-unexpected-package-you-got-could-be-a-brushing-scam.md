So, you open your front door one fine morning, and there it is—a package! But wait, you didn’t order anything. Could it be a secret admirer? A surprise gift from a distant relative? Or maybe... just maybe... a sneaky scam! Welcome to the world of brushing scams, where packages arrive unannounced and mystery reigns supreme!

**The Unsolicited Surprise:**

This bizarre phenomenon often starts with an unexpected package that seems to pop out of nowhere. It typically looks like it's from a familiar company like Amazon or some obscure business you've never heard of. Inside, you might find random items like baby wipes, toothpaste, or even seeds. No, it's not a hint from the universe to start gardening or improve your dental hygiene—it's likely a scam!

**Why the Random Package?**

In this strange saga, scammers send these packages to your address to "validate" the delivery. Then, they use your name to post fake glowing reviews about their products online, boosting their own sales. Basically, they’re trying to brush up their business by brushing you off with random goods!

**Beware the QR Code:**

Sometimes, these packages come with a note featuring a QR code. Its message might urge you to scan it to discover the sender's identity or to process a return. But beware! Scanning that code could lead you to a phishing website designed to swipe your sensitive info like credit card numbers, usernames, or passwords. Yikes!

**What to Do If You’re a Brushing Scam Target:**

1. **Change Your Passwords:** First, change your passwords on all online shopping accounts. This is your frontline defense in case your accounts have been compromised.
   
2. **Contact the Marketplace:** If the package came from a known online marketplace, get in touch with them. They can investigate and potentially boot the shady seller.

3. **Monitor Your Credit:** Keep an eye on your credit by checking it weekly for free at [AnnualCreditReport.com](https://www.annualcreditreport.com). Look for any signs of sneaky identity theft.

4. **Know Your Rights:** According to the law, you don't have to return unordered items, nor do you have to pay for them. But, it's wise to be cautious about using the product since you don’t know its origin.

5. **Report the Scam:** If you suspect a brushing scam, let the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) know by reporting it at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov).

Remember, while surprise packages might sound exciting, when they’re unexpected and unsolicited, it's best to proceed with caution and a healthy dose of skepticism!
