Ah, health insurance scams—the uninvited guests at the party we call "Open Enrollment." Just when you're ready to find the perfect plan for Medicare or the Marketplace, these scammers swoop in, like mosquitoes at a summer BBQ. So, let’s swat them away with some handy tips and a sprinkle of humor!

### Beware the Evil Twins of the Internet

Ever notice how some websites look eerily like the real deal but feel a bit...off? Scammers are the masters of mimicry, crafting web addresses that resemble government sites. They even throw in official-looking logos and keywords to make you think you've hit the jackpot with Healthcare.gov or Medicare.gov. But before you dive in, remember: just because it quacks like a duck doesn’t mean it’s not a very sneaky goose.

### The Perils of Clickbait

You know how those ads promise you'll lose 20 pounds by eating nothing but chocolate cake? Yeah, health insurance scams are kind of like that. Scammers pay big bucks to pop up at the top of your search results, hoping you'll click on their link and find yourself knee-deep in a scammy plan. And, spoiler alert, these plans aren’t really health insurance but a one-way ticket to medical identity theft.

### Be a Detective, Not a Victim

Here’s your first clue: type the website directly into your browser. For Medicare, it's Medicare.gov, and the magic number is 800-633-4227. For all things Marketplace, visit Healthcare.gov or call 800-318-2596. Think of it as the digital equivalent of checking for the “.gov” label, because a .gov ending is like finding the golden ticket in a candy bar—only less sticky.

### Ads, Ads Everywhere

A quick tip from your friendly neighborhood detective: look for the tiny "Ad" or "Sponsored" labels next to search results. Scrolling past these paid ads is like passing on that last slice of pizza—hard, but oh so necessary. Aim for that sweet, sweet .gov ending, and you’re golden.

### Help is Just a Call Away

Need help comparing costs and plans? Fear not! Your trusty sidekicks, the State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) and the local Senior Medicare Patrol, are here to save the day. And for ACA plans, Healthcare.gov has a “Find Local Help” feature that’s better than a superhero hotline.

### Stay Informed, Stay Safe

For a VIP pass to the world of healthcare scams, join a roundtable conversation with the FTC, Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services, and the Senior Medicare Patrol on September 29, 2026, at 1 pm ET. Because who doesn’t love a good chat about scams over a cup of virtual tea?

### Report the Rascals

Think you’ve spotted a scam? Tell the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, or dial up your state attorney general. If Medicare’s in the mix, report it at Medicare.gov or call 800-633-4227. Together, we can show these scammers that we mean business!

Remember, knowledge is power, and laughter is the best armor. Happy (and safe) insurance hunting!
