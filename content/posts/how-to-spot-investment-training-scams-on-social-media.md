Welcome to the digital age, where your social media feed is a buffet of fancy cars, exotic getaways, and promises of a millionaire lifestyle—all courtesy of trading online. "Quit your job! Get rich fast!" they shout, conveniently leaving out the part where trading is riskier than betting your life savings on a three-legged horse named Lucky.

Here's a tale of caution involving International Markets Live (IML), also known as IM Mastery Academy and IYOVIA. The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) recently took these folks to court for pulling a fast one on young people with shiny promises. According to the FTC, IML had "educators" who were about as credentialed as your cat, and they used bogus earnings claims to lure people into their investment training scheme. But fear not, justice was served! The company was shut down, its leaders are turning over assets worth over $90 million, and they're banned from selling any trading training or investment opportunities in the future. So, no more money-making magic shows from them!

Now, before you consider diving into the world of online trading, here's a handy guide to keep your wallet safe and your sanity intact:

1. **Beware of the "Guaranteed Money" Trap:** If someone promises you can make a fortune with little to no risk, they might as well be selling unicorns. Spoiler: they're a scammer.

2. **Educators or Impersonators?** Anyone claiming they can teach you to trade successfully without any proof of success or credentials is likely more con artist than Wall Street wizard. Run!

3. **Do Your Homework:** Before you invest in any "opportunity," research the company, its officials, and promoters. A quick search with their name plus "review," "scam," or "fraud" might save you a world of pain. And don't forget to check with your state attorney general for any complaints.

4. **Phone a Friend:** Take your time and talk to someone you trust about any coaching program. Asking questions and getting a second opinion can be the difference between smart investing and eating ramen noodles for the rest of the month.

5. **Report the Rascals:** If you spot a scam, report it at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://www.reportfraud.ftc.gov). It's like doing the community a solid and ensuring these scammers get a taste of justice.

So next time you see someone on social media flaunting their "success" in trading, remember: if it sounds too good to be true, it probably is. Arm yourself with knowledge, and don't let the scammers hit the jackpot with your hard-earned cash!
