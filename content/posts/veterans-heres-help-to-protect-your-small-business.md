Hey there, veteran business heroes! Just when you thought your days of dodging threats ended with your service, the modern world throws cybercriminals and scammers into the mix. But fear not—I've got some friendly advice to help you stand guard over your small business.

First off, your best defense is an informed team. So, let's start with a roll call of resources that’ll have your staff spotting scams faster than you can say "cybersecurity."

### Scam Spotting 101

Head over to [ftc.gov/SmallBusiness](https://www.ftc.gov/SmallBusiness) for the FTC’s "Scams and Your Small Business" article. It’s packed with info on common scams targeting businesses, like those pesky fake invoices (they're not bills, they're bogus), suspicious tech support calls, and those oh-so-persistent business impostors. Share this with your team and turn them into scam-detecting ninjas!

### Cybersecurity Crash Course

Want to dive deeper into your digital defenses? Check out [ftc.gov/cybersecurity](https://www.ftc.gov/cybersecurity). This treasure trove of tips will help you and your staff tackle phishing scams, email authentication, and even prepare a plan for that dreaded data breach. Because, let’s face it, “surprise” breaches are about as welcome as a skunk at a picnic.

### Culture of Security

Creating a culture of security isn’t just about hanging motivational posters with eagles and sunsets. Train your team to keep passwords and sensitive info off email (no matter how much that "manager" insists). Encourage them to speak up if they smell a scam—teamwork makes the cybersecurity dream work!

### Plan Like a Pro

Draft a battle plan for saving data, running your biz, and notifying customers if a data breach does occur. Think of it as your company’s superhero cape, ready to swoosh into action when needed.

### Stay Alert, Stay Ahead

Keep your ear to the ground by subscribing to the FTC’s [Business Blog](https://www.ftc.gov/business-blog) and [Consumer Alerts](https://www.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts). These updates will keep you one step ahead of the scammers, who, let’s be honest, have about as much charm as a cactus in a balloon store.

And remember, if you do spot a scam, report it faster than a squirrel on a power line at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://ReportFraud.ftc.gov). Sharing is caring, and your report could save others from a similar fate.

Stay safe out there, veteran business champions. With these tips, your business will be more secure than a top-secret government bunker.
